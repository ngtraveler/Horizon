# Evaluation goal

Evaluate how much a timely item matters to a reader who works in or follows the leisure marine industry: yachting and sailing, boatbuilding and refit, marine equipment and marine electronics, marinas, charter, and the regulation and commercial maritime context around them.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Industry-moving.** A new platform or model line from a major builder that changes a segment; a merger, acquisition, or insolvency that reshapes supply; a regulatory or certification change that forces redesign or recertification (CE/RCD, emissions, USCG safety alert or recall); a genuinely new propulsion, battery, or navigation platform other vendors will adopt; a casualty investigation that changes design, crewing, or operating rules.
- **7-8: High value.** Concrete product launches with specifications, pricing, and availability; credible measured performance or comparison data; order, delivery, or yard-capacity news with numbers; quarterly results and guidance from listed marine companies; boat-show launches and award results; marina, dry-stack, or infrastructure projects with capacity figures; refit and new-build contracts.
- **5-6: Worth knowing.** Incremental model-year updates, appointments, small-yard projects, event previews, market commentary without new data, software firmware updates, routine class or club news.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Promotional posts, teasers, listicles, "best of" galleries with no specifications, press releases that restate marketing language, minor racing results with no design or safety dimension.
- **0-2: Noise.** Off-topic content, celebrity sightings, duplicated press releases, content-farm rewrites, clickbait, or items whose boat reference is incidental.

# Evaluation guidance

Judge by consequence for the people who build, sell, specify, survey, service, or operate boats. Reward concrete facts: model and product names, length and power, price, certification class, delivery dates, unit volumes, financial figures, named suppliers. Treat a vendor's own claim as a claim, not as a measured result.

Downgrade luxury-lifestyle framing, broker listings with no news value, and racing coverage whose only content is a result table. A racing item scores higher when it documents a design, materials, or safety development that transfers to production boats.

Popularity and engagement are weak signals here: a forum thread with 200 comments about a gelcoat defect can outweigh a widely syndicated press release, but only if the thread reports a specific, repeatable problem.

Use three to five specific tags, such as `electric propulsion`, `marine electronics`, `refit`, `RCD certification`, `superyacht order`, `marina capacity`, `sailing safety`.
